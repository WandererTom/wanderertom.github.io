*Luminar 113 (Morning), Edenfall*  
[[@Ichoren]], [[@Zharek]], [[@Jack]], [[@Splatts]], [[@Cove-Candle]]  

The party stepped down into a dark corridor, descending wide stone steps to a golden archway. Here it was inscribed with the words, "*Turn now, forget this place. Doom awaits those who meddle in matters they do not understand.*"

Taking a breath, they continued on. Beneath, they found themselves in a stone corridor at the end of which stood a large stone statue of a dwarf clutching an axe in a guard-like position. The party soon noticed the mangled body that lay on the ground beside the statue: a person garbed in a black cloak - a member of [[The Cabal of the Sundered Stone|The Cabal]]. [[@Splatts]] noticed there was blood on the statue's axe, and soon realised how this person had met their end.

[[@Ichoren]] led the party forwards, but they were compelled to stop by the statue as it scraped into life. A stone golem guarded the door behind it, demanding the passphrase from the visitors.

After some thought and conversation, [[@Jack]] sensed the place had a spiritual connection of some kind, and [[@Zharek]] repeated the phrase the ghost had said to [[@Ichoren]] on the road. To their surprise, the golem allowed them to pass. They entered the stone doorway, having found it slightly ajar. 

Inside they came to a large chamber. A huge, golden door stood opposite, firmly locked. Four paths led from the main chamber, a statue standing beside the entrance to each.

[[@Cove-Candle]] thought he heard footsteps from behind, but the party saw nothing when they investigated.

Choosing one of the paths, the party soon found themed themselves staring into a chamber filled with writhing vines and huge spore-producing plants. At the other side was a locked gate, barring the way to a small anti-chamber containing an unlit brazier. In the centre of the main chamber was a lever. They realised they needed to use the lever to unlock the gate. [[@Splatts]] investigated and learned the vines writhed towards him but retreated from fire. [[@Ichoren]] used this knowledge to make his way through, covering his face to protect himself from the spores. He made it to the lever, opened the gate, and lit the fire. Back in the main chamber, the party saw a lit rune beside the room's statue had extinguished, leaving three more to go.

The party made it through the next three chambers, finding each contained a different challenge to unlock the gate.

[[@Jack]] defied death to balance his way across precarious moving platforms and avoid falling to a spiked pit below (unlike the poor soul clad in a black cloak whose body lay impaled down in the dark).

[[@Cove-Candle]] swam across a water-filled room only to be eaten by a giant octopus. [[@Zharek]] tried to electrocute it but zapped [[@Cove-Candle]] by mistake. [[@Ichoren]] launched into the water and brutalised the creature as it tried to swim away with its prey, and freed [[@Cove-Candle]] from its bowels.

[[@Zharek]] and [[@Jack]] risked immolation, hopping over lava and dodging fire to light the final brazier.

With the golden door unlocked, they entered the chamber beyond. Inside was a wondrous sight: a large room filled with shimmering light that emanated from a gemstone resting on a central plinth. [[@Jack]] immediately recognised the [[Spark of Asantia]] and collapsed to his knees and wept.

Before it was a stone table containing a scroll, and just behind that a plinth held a golden crown.

They read the scroll and found it contained words written by [[Alzar]] the great wizard long ago.

> [!quote] The Fall of Alethyria
> *Set down by Alzar, High Mage of Vadoria, on the first triad of the New Age.*
> 
> I inscribe these words as warning. I shall leave this place, and pray that eyes never again fall upon that which lies within this chamber.
> 
> I stand in the shadow of the Old Age. The lands around me are broken. We know only death. But it was not always this way.
> 
> There are those who know of the Age of Radiance, when the world was yet unbroken. Then, the Goddesses walked among their children. The mountains sang with their breath. The rivers carried their whispers. The very air shimmered with the living weave of magic, unbound and unspoiled. In those days dwelled the Ancients—mortal keepers of the sacred harmony—who did not command the lands, but listened to them, with purity of heart and humility.
> 
> For this Goddesses loved them dearly.
> 
> So profound was that love that the Dawnmother, first among the divine, wrought a gift of surpassing grace: a diadem of living light, crowned with twelve jewels, each kindled with the soul of her radiant daughters. 
> 
> Not as a weapon was it given.  
> Not as a crown of dominion.  
> But as a covenant.
> 
> Through it, the Ancients touched the currents of magic as one might touch still water—gently, reverently—never to master, but to coexist. Their minds were opened to possibilities never again revealed.
> 
> Yet all golden ages must fade.
> 
> The Ancients waned, as twilight follows day. Their voices grew few, their cities fell silent, and at last they turned from these lands, bearing with them only memory and love. Here, in this sanctum, they laid the Dawnmother’s Diadem to rest, sealing it in sacred silence, that it might sleep until the Goddesses themselves called it home.
> 
> But these lands did not remain empty.
> 
> In time, a new people claimed them, their hearts unknowing and untampered with reverence. And in an age of rising crowns and restless ambition, one among them—a mage of great hunger—found the hidden path to this chamber.
> 
> What is known… is that they came.
> 
> They crossed the threshold unbidden.  
> They beheld the Diadem.  
> And they took it.
> 
> No prayer was spoken.  
> No rite observed.  
> No understanding held.
> 
> And so the covenant was broken.
> 
> Urged by a desperate ruler, and blinded by the promise of power, the mage sought to chain the Dawnmother's light—to turn heaven’s grace toward the ends of war. The Diadem, which had known only harmony, was forced to fracture its own song.
> 
> And all screamed.
> 
> Thus came the cataclysm now named The Rending.
> 
> Magic tore itself apart. Light devoured shadow, and shadow consumed light. Flesh became ash.  The sky wept. Stone cracked asunder. The Diadem burned with a fury no mortal soul could withstand—its jewels shattered, its essence unbound, its song reduced to a cry of ruin.
> 
> Alethyria fell. 
> 
> And all that was became less.
> 
> Silence at last returned, but the lands were mortally wounded.
> 
> From the ashes, the rulers swore an oath to bind the ages—a binding of peoples, and a promise to never again unleash the ruin that befell them. Thus the Diadem was unmade; its sacred jewels scattered across the farthest reaches of the lands. Each stone was hidden; disguised, and entrusted to a few to hold in secret, ignorant of their true origin. The Diadem was struck from record, never to be uttered of again. Its memory lies buried beneath fear, known only to those who forged the Pact or stood beside those who did.
> 
> And this place—this last witness—shall be sealed, entombed beneath a broken mountain, that none might ever again trespass upon what should not be touched.
> 
> So I have written.  
> So I have hidden.
> 
> May these words never find the light.
> 
> But if you who read this stand now within these forsaken halls, then know this truth, lest it be forgotten by the world above:
> 
> The Dawnmother’s light is not a gift to be wielded by any.  
> It is a grace to be honoured, to be bestowed upon the worthy.
> 
> To claim it as a weapon is to unmake the world once more.

[[@Ichoren]] stepped into the radiant light, feeling the warmth of the [[Spark of Asantia|Spark]] wash over his body. He lifted the gemstone from its plinth, taking it. [[@Zharek]] collected the diadem and placed it into his rucksack.

Beyond this chamber the party found another door that led them to a smaller chamber. Here a large portal crackled with energy. Beside it sat a dial etched with the numbers 1-12, and on the wall a simple carving with 12 marked points. They realised it was a simple map of [[Alethyria]] and wasted no time in choosing their destination: [[Nethirial]].

On a nearby table they found a tome containing instructions from [[Alzar]]. It detailed how portal travel was once used regularly, but since [[The Rending]] the magical fabrics that it relied upon were temperamental. The party knew they could travel through but there were risks: they might arrive at an unintended location or, if they were terribly unlucky, find themselves torn apart in the magical stream.

They decided not to continue their journey on foot and make the most of the opportunity before them. Despite the risks, they set the dial and one by one stepped through into the light...

Just as [[@Cove-Candle]] entered he turned to see a figure cloaked in black was watching from the shadows of the corridor behind them...

... the stream took them, throwing them into turbulence and tearing at their physical forms. Pain gripped them, but they made it through. They found themselves emerge into a chamber filled with light and warmth...
